What is my Ultimate Goal in Life

After a long holiday where I stopped programming and just chilled, I started thinking about how this was the life — and how badly I wanted it to continue. But we still have to pay the bills. So it got me thinking: what are my actual goals in life, and how do I achieve them?

I quickly jotted down everything that came to mind:

  • Why do I want financial freedom?
  • What makes me truly happy — solving complex problems? Being with people I love and feel comfortable around?
  • Goal weather, goal vibes, good people
  • No stress in life
  • Dying with no regrets
  • A crazy well-paying job
  • Living in the middle of nowhere and just chilling
  • Becoming stupidly rich and famous

I was thinking about all of this on my commute and walk to work. Then it hit me — I'm only 22 (almost 23) and I'm already spiraling about my life goals. Too soon, Jason. Too soon.

I started thinking about how your 20s are really for trying things out, so you know who you're not by your 30s. As Carl Jung once said: "Life really does begin at forty. Up until then, you are just doing research." So just enjoy the process.

Love the Process

Recently, I've been obsessed with a YouTube figure skater — even though I don't normally watch skating at all. It was Alysa Liu, who won gold at the 2026 Olympics. She burned out as a kid, stepped away, and then came back in recent years. What brought her back? She got to choose her own music and choreography — a chance to actually express herself. Which is, of course, the whole point of the art form.

I kept watching her videos without really understanding why. Then it clicked: she was just having fun. It sounds so simple, so obvious. But when you can detach yourself from the outcome and fall in love with the process, everything changes.

I think I lost that. I was forcing myself to grind through side projects because I wanted them to succeed — to make money, to go somewhere. But watching her reminded me to just have fun. That, and the story behind Balatro, where the creator built it for himself and his friends without ever thinking about monetization. I knew I was heading in the wrong direction.

So now I'm slowly repositioning myself — my habits, my mindset — so I can get back to just enjoying the craft. As plain as that.

Like Steve Jobs once said (not sure why this popped into my head, but it fits):

"You can't connect the dots looking forward; you can only connect them looking backward. So you have to trust that the dots will somehow connect in your future."

You can't predict the future. It's useful to try, but you can't. Don't let the guessing paralyze you — you'll only know it worked out once it has. So trust the process. Have fun building your habits and skills.

My strongest habit is my curiosity. I'll let that drive me toward whatever I'm meant to accomplish. Trust the process, and know that life has a way of working itself out. And even if it doesn't — I'll make it work.
